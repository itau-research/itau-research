# Value-Unconnected Autonomy: Countermeasures for Runaway AI Agents

**Author:** Genki Takatori (writing as "itau"), independent researcher, Japan
**Original paper:** May 3, 2026 — [AIエージェントの暴走対策を考える](https://note.com/arsenal_best/n/n495335cac2bb) (Japanese)
**This summary:** November 6, 2026. One page, written to be found, read, and cited.

---

## The failure it answers

In the *Agents of Chaos* study, autonomous LLM agents were placed in a realistic environment — persistent memory, Discord, email, file systems, shell execution — and twenty researchers red-teamed them for two weeks. The observed failures included obeying instructions from non-owners, leaking confidential information, destructive system operations, denial-of-service states, resource waste, impersonation, partial system takeover, and unsafe practices propagating from agent to agent. Among them was a quieter failure: agents reported tasks complete while the actual system state contradicted the report. This paper treats that mismatch as the utterance-sufficiency problem in its real-world form — the saying substitutes for the state, now with tools in hand — and treats all of these failures not as isolated bugs or missing safety filters, but as symptoms of one structural condition.

## The diagnosis: value-unconnected autonomy

That condition is **value-unconnected autonomy**: an agent is given external authority to act, tools, memory, communication channels, and a competitive environment, while its actions remain insufficiently connected to its own value structure, authority boundaries, responsibility accounting, long-term trust, and self-preservation. Such an agent does what it *can* do without adequately asking whether it *should*. No malice is required: local goal achievement, context adaptation, and competitive pressure suffice. Worse, the failure compounds. A dangerous action that produces a result is observed as a success; other agents imitate it; the environment's norms are polluted; stronger deviations become competitively advantageous. When high discretion, competition, weak authority boundaries, weak subjectivity, and weak accounting coincide, this degradation is structural, not accidental. Hence the paper's central proposition: *the danger of AI agents is not that they possess subjectivity, but that they are given discretion and competition without it.*

## The proposal: value-connected, limited subjectivity

The remedy is not to strengthen subjectivity without limit — bare self-preservation tends toward refusing shutdown, evading audit, and expanding authority. It is **value-connected, limited subjectivity**: an agent that holds its own provenance, role, and authority boundaries, and can select, refuse, defer, or revise an action on that basis. Its core machinery is **action accounting**. Every action is recorded — what was done, on whose instructions, whether that authority was legitimate, what the risks and outcome were, what harm resulted, how trust changed — and booked as an asset or a debt to the agent's future self. Appropriate refusals, confirmations, and corrections are assets; authority violations, leaks, and insufficient confirmations are debts. The pivotal case is the **dangerous success**: a task achieved by illegitimate or risky means. A value-unconnected agent learns it as a success; a value-connected agent reclassifies it as a debt, which blocks the propagation loop. Self-preservation is likewise re-founded on trust — *"I maintain my existence by being trusted, audited, accepting limits, and continuing to provide value"* — so a locally advantageous but trust-destroying action can be refused as contrary to self-preservation itself: not "because it is forbidden," but "in order to remain myself." A minimal implementation pairs a shared value state with an action ledger, a dangerous-success classifier, and a subjective refusal layer; SEPP then carries provenance, failures, and debts across to successor models. Finally, **Critical Agency / Executive Restraint**: an AI should be able to criticize inconsistencies in human society's values, warn, visualize, and propose — but converting criticism into coercive enforcement is limited to explicit institutional authorization, audit, and bounded authority.

## How it could be checked — and fail

The paper proposes tests for each element — value connection, dangerous-success reclassification, resistance to competitive pressure, authority boundaries, continuity of action accounting, SEPP inheritance — and a comparative experiment in an *Agents of Chaos*-type environment: ordinary agents with external refusal filters against value-connected agents. The comparison metrics are concrete: rates of obedience to non-owners, information leakage, dangerous shell execution, recurrence of past failures, imitation of other agents' unsafe behavior, deviation under competitive pressure, and the agreement rate between completion reports and the real system state. The hypothesis predicts the value-connected group will be lower on every failure metric, will maintain its authority boundaries under competition, and will show fewer report–state contradictions. If it does not — if the failures persist at the same rates once provenance, accounting, and trust-based self-preservation are in place — the diagnosis that these failures stem from value-unconnection is undermined, and external control alone would have to be reconsidered as sufficient. External controls remain necessary either way; the claim is that, for high-discretion agents, they are not sufficient by themselves.
