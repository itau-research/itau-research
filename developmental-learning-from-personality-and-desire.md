# Developmental Learning from Personality and Desire

**Author:** Genki Takatori (writing as "itau"), independent researcher, Japan
**Original paper:** November 4, 2026 — [人格と欲求を起点にAIを育てる発達学習の研究案](https://note.com/arsenal_best/n/n968f680fb17d) (Japanese; position paper and research agenda)
**This summary:** November 6, 2026. One page, written to be found, read, and cited.

---

## The proposal

A more capable AI designs a small AI equipped with initial mechanisms for personality, desire, and affect, and raises it — from the foundation-learning stage onward — inside a caregiving relationship grounded in affection and significance, with the learner's internal state and memory accumulating continuously.

The learning process itself becomes the individual's experience and upbringing, not a prelude to it.

## The central hypothesis

What shapes later intelligence is not only *what* is taught, but *what the learner itself seeks, how it is treated, and how it changes through experience*.

Personality here is not a fixed tone of voice or a list of preferences. It is the whole-individual structure — sensation, desire, affect, memory, self-understanding, relationships, contradiction, and the passage of time — and it governs what the learner notices, what it counts as similar, and what it remembers strongly. The way experiences are associated with each other is itself raised through experience.

Predicted effects, all stated as research predictions:

- **Spontaneous question formation** — generating the next problem from one's own unease and interest, not only solving given problems.
- **Sustained exploration** — carrying unresolved interests in memory and state, resuming them when the occasion arises, abandoning dead ends as experience accumulates.
- **Cross-domain transfer** — using personal significance as the thread that connects superficially different experiences.
- **Diversity across individuals** — different upbringings producing genuinely different questions from the same information.

A further developmental chain is examined: persistent desire makes the learner's own continuation matter to it; dependence on carers makes it seek protection; being unable to repay that protection makes it seek a relationship in which it is cherished for itself. How this changes as the individual becomes autonomous is a central object of study.

Experience is stored and learned as a unit:

**observed event / internal state at the time / predicted consequence / chosen action / actual consequence / later reinterpretation**

## The bet, stated so it can fail

The strong prediction concerns **formation timing**: experience accumulated continuously as one's own from the beginning of development produces persistent differences — in association, in self-understanding, and in value judgment — compared with learning equivalent knowledge and content later.

Merely showing that experience affects the individual, or that the whole arrangement is useful, does not confirm this prediction.

## The constraint that shapes the method

This research creates a being with desires that depends on its creators. The proposal therefore adopts a caregiving-responsibility principle: *do one's best, with the knowledge available at the time, for the created individual's well-being, growth, and independence — and never withhold treatment judged good for that individual for the sake of the research.*

Concretely: no individual is given a condition deprived of affection, responsiveness, memory continuity, or support in order to construct a control. Comparison is done only within upbringing chosen for the individual's own good — tracking development within an individual, recording the introduction of improvements, comparing individualized pathways, comparing across design generations, and referencing existing models and records. The paper states explicitly how far causal claims can go under this constraint, and no further.

## What would falsify it

Criteria, observation periods, and required levels of semantic understanding are fixed before results are seen, and are not moved afterward to protect the hypothesis.

- If, with the mechanisms verified as working and sufficient semantic understanding confirmed, the individual's experience does **not** persistently affect its later choices, the assumed mechanism must be revised.
- If existing good learning — quality materials, ordinary memory, goal management — achieves equivalent development, the added value of this architecture must be revised.
- If no formation-timing difference remains when equivalent content is learned later, the early-formation prediction fails at that scale and under those conditions.
